# Phishing Email Analysis Project

## 📖 Overview
This project focuses on analyzing real-world phishing emails to identify **key indicators**, understand **attack methods**, and provide **prevention guidelines**.  
The goal is to help individuals and organizations recognize phishing attempts and respond effectively.

We use actual phishing samples (Coinbase impersonation, Microsoft account alert, Ninja Foodi prize scam) to illustrate how attackers operate and how users can protect themselves.

---

- Collect and document phishing email samples.

- Explain phishing attacks in **non-technical terms** for accessibility.
- Provide **Do’s and Don’ts** for prevention.
- Outline **response steps** if someone has already been phished.

## 🛠️ Phishing Case Studies
### 2. Microsoft Account Alert
- **Indicator:** Sender domain mismatch (`access-accsecurity.com`), reply-to Gmail address.
- **Attack Method:** Fake security alert leading to a phishing page for Microsoft login.
- **Emotional Trigger:** Fear (account compromise).

- ### 1. Coinbase Impersonation
- **Indicator:** Fake domain (`shangriladogchew.com`), urgent subject line about phone number change.
- **Attack Method:** Tricks user into clicking a fake login page to steal cryptocurrency credentials.
- **Emotional Trigger:** Fear (account takeover).


### 3. Ninja Foodi Prize Scam
- **Indicator:** “You’ve won” subject line, mismatched domains (`ganmorfan.com`, `mcwwh.com`).
- **Attack Method:** Fake prize claim form to steal personal/financial data.
- **Emotional Trigger:** Excitement (winning a prize).

## ✅ Prevention Guidelines

### Do’s
- Verify sender domains carefully.
- Hover over links before clicking.
- Access accounts directly via official websites/apps.
- Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA).
- Report suspicious emails to providers/companies.
- Trust instincts — if it feels off, it probably is.

### Don’ts
- Don’t click links or open attachments in suspicious emails.
- Don’t reply to suspicious senders (especially free email accounts).
- Don’t share personal or financial info via email.
- Don’t trust urgent/emotional language without verification.
- Don’t assume logos or formatting mean legitimacy.

---

## 🚨 What To Do If You’ve Been Phished
1. **Change passwords immediately** (on the real site, not via the email link).
2. **Enable MFA** for extra protection.
3. **Check accounts for unusual activity** and report suspicious logins.
4. **Run antivirus/security scans** on your device.
5. **Notify your bank/credit card provider** if financial info was shared.
6. **Report the phishing email** to the company or email provider.

---

## 📌 Key Takeaway
Phishing attacks rely on **emotional manipulation**:
- Fear (security alerts, account changes).
- Excitement (prize winnings).  

The best defense is **awareness, caution, and quick action** if compromised.
